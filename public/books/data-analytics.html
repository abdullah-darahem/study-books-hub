1.1

Why Data Analytics? Foundations & Case Studies

ليه الـ Data Analytics؟ الأساسات والـ case studies

Data is the input to nearly every modern system. Traditional software took user input and produced output based on fixed logic. Today's intelligent systems (recommendation engines, self-driving cars) derive their logic from data itself.

As a data scientist or engineer you are building data-driven pipelines that transform raw, chaotic bytes into structured knowledge that powers decisions.

The foundations of Data Analytics (intersection of 3 disciplines)
DisciplineWhat it brings
Computer Scienceefficient data structures, algorithms, distributed computing, and databases
Statistics & Mathematicsprobability, linear algebra, statistical inference, and optimization

Case Study 1 · E-commerce: isolated vs integrated analysis

Problem: an e-commerce company noticed a sustained decline in quarterly sales revenue. Traditional diagnostics treat it as a commercial or marketing failure, leading to misplaced strategic interventions.

Isolated (Non-Holistic)Integrated (Holistic)
looks only at the sales report and finds that sales declinedintegrates sales + website behavior + marketing campaigns + inventory + shipping + customer complaints + competitor prices
examines a single partexamines the entire system

Discovery: the cause was not the market but a shipping delay in a specific region → more cancellations and a decline in trust. Results: a broader, more accurate vision and revealing hidden relationships between departments.

Case Study 2 · The Moneyball Framework (value creation & feature selection)
  • 2002: the Oakland A's had a payroll of $40 million vs the New York Yankees with more than $125 million. How do you win when you can't afford star talent?
  • GM Billy Beane and analyst Paul DePodesta replaced scouts' gut feelings and intuition with a data-driven approach.
  • Feature selection: scouts overvalued flashy stats like Batting Average. Analytics showed On-Base Percentage (OBP) (how often a player reaches base by any means) was the single best predictor of scoring runs and winning.
  • Arbitrage (market inefficiencies): high-OBP players were undervalued because they lacked athletic looks or celebrity status → bought at a fraction of market cost.
  • Results: 103 wins (tied the Yankees for the best record) spending less than one-third of the Yankees' budget per win; an American League record of 20 consecutive wins; two years later the Boston Red Sox used the same model to win their first championship in 86 years (World Series 2004, first since 1918).

Avoiding vanity metricsfrom player popularity/style to raw OBPconversion rates & customer lifetime value, not website traffic or likes
Removing human biasobjective statistics instead of subjective scouting intuitiondata-backed hiring, marketing and operational decisions
Leveling the playing fieldoutsmart richer competitors with data insightsstartups and lean teams outmaneuver industry giants via overlooked opportunities

Data Analysis vs Data Analytics & the Analytics Spectrum

الفرق بين Analysis و Analytics وأنواع التحليل الأربعة

Data Analysis (Micro)Data Analytics (Macro)
Definitiona specific activity within the domain: inspecting, cleaning, transforming and modeling a specific existing dataset to answer precise questionsa broad field and complete system: collecting, storing, organizing, analyzing, predicting, and making automatic decisions
FocusRetrospective (past and present data)Prospective (future trends and optimization)
Core question"What happened, and why did it happen?""What will happen next, and what is the best course of action?"
Outputs / scopestatic reports, dashboards, summary statistics, answers to historical queriescovers all four types: descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, prescriptive

Technical definition: from a computational standpoint, data analytics is a multi-stage transformation workflow (pipeline). It takes raw data as input and produces insights, predictions, recommendations, or decisions as output. The pipeline is not linear; it is highly iterative, with constant feedback loops between stages.

The Analytics Spectrum (rising complexity and value)
TypeQuestionTechnical approachExample
DescriptiveWhat happened?aggregation, summary statistics, OLAP cubes, data visualizationdashboard of daily active users (DAU) over the last month
DiagnosticWhy did it happen?drill-down, cohort analysis, correlation, root-cause analysisDAU dropped after a specific API deployment
PredictiveWhat will happen?supervised ML (regression, classification), time-series forecastingpredict which users will churn next week (logistic regression)
PrescriptiveWhat should we do?optimization, simulation, reinforcement learning, A/B testingrecommend the optimal discount per user to maximize retention at minimum cost
Descriptivewhat?→Diagnosticwhy?→Predictivewhat next?→Prescriptivewhat to do?

Moving up from descriptive to prescriptive increases both technical complexity and business value. Descriptive is the baseline; prescriptive is the frontier. Your job is to move up this stack.

Phase 0 Goal, the 4 Layers & the 5 Data Characteristics

هدف Phase 0 والطبقات الأربعة وخصائص الداتا

Technical goal: establishing a unified, secure, and reproducible computational foundation to store, manage, track, and process data efficiently before executing any analytical operations. Think of it as building the analytics platform, not just installing tools. It should support the entire lifecycle, from raw data ingestion to model deployment and monitoring.

1 · Data Storage+2 · Processing Engines+3 · Version Control (Git)+4 · Reproducibility & Environment Isolation

Distributed & Cloud Storage · Data Warehouse, Lake, Lakehouse

التخزين الموزّع والسحابي والـ Warehouse والـ Lake والـ Lakehouse

Distributed & cloud storage holds massive datasets that exceed a single machine's capacity. Data is split and distributed across servers or cloud locations for durability, redundancy, and high-speed retrieval. Examples: HDFS, Amazon S3, Azure Blob, Google Cloud Storage.

Real-life example: YouTube / Shahid 4K video

One 2-hour 4K video ≈ 100 GB. On one server: the disk fills fast, 50,000 viewers crash it (network & read bottlenecks), and a disk failure loses the video forever. Distributed storage solves it with:

MechanismWhat happens
Splitting & distributionthe 100 GB file → 100 segments of 1 GB spread across 10 servers
Redundancy (replication)each segment stored on 3 servers (replication factor = 3); if server #5 fails, the segment is read from server #8 with no interruption
Parallel readingdozens of servers stream segments in parallel to thousands of users
Best forLimitationsAnalogy
Big Data, data lakes, backups, cheap long-term storage, raw data before processingnot a database by itself: needs a query engine (Spark, Presto, Athena); higher latency; often no full ACID; security needs careful configurationa very large warehouse of raw data that needs tools to search it
Data WarehouseData LakeData Lakehouse
Dataonly structured, cleaned & transformed via ETLall types in raw native formatall types in open-format files + a governance layer (transaction log)
SchemaSchema-on-Write (define before ingestion)Schema-on-Read (apply when queried)structure + validation on top of the lake
Strengthsblazing-fast SQL, high quality, ACIDmax flexibility, low-cost petabyte storagesingle source of truth for BI and ML; no duplication; cheap + governed
Limitationshigh cost; can't handle video, audio, raw logsslower reporting; can become a "Data Swamp" without governance—

عالشرح بالعربيمخزن كبير محتاج أدوات
🎬 مثال يوتيوب خطوة بخطوة
  1. الفيديو حجمه 100 GB، نقسمه 100 حتة كل حتة 1 GB.
  2. نوزّع الحتت على 10 سيرفرات، وكل حتة متخزنة على 3 سيرفرات (replication factor = 3).
  3. لو سيرفر وقع، الحتة بتتقري من سيرفر تاني من غير ما المشاهد يحس.
  4. ولما آلاف الناس يتفرجوا، السيرفرات بتبعت الحتت بالتوازي فمفيش زحمة.
  • التخزين الموزّع مش database لوحده، محتاج محرك استعلام زي Spark أو Presto أو Athena.
  • Warehouse = داتا نضيفة ومنظمة جاهزة للتقارير، بتتنظّم قبل ما تدخل (Schema-on-Write).
  • Lake = كل حاجة خام زي ما هي، بتتنظّم وقت القراية (Schema-on-Read). لو مافيش تنظيم بيبقى «مستنقع» (Data Swamp).
  • Lakehouse = الاتنين مع بعض: رخيص زي الـ Lake ومنظم وفيه ACID زي الـ Warehouse.
فخ: «Data Lake uses Schema-on-Write» ← غلط، الـ Lake Schema-on-Read والـ Warehouse هو اللي Schema-on-Write.
فخ: «Snowflake is a Data Lake» ← غلط، Snowflake و Redshift و BigQuery أمثلة Warehouse.

Version control (Git): a "time machine" or save point that tracks every code change. It provides change tracking (revert to a working version), collaboration (many analysts without overwriting each other), and schema evolution (tracks data definitions and pipeline logic too).

Reproducibility & environment isolation: a "sealed laboratory" so code runs identically on any computer.

  • Virtual environments (venv, Conda): isolated spaces with exact library versions → no dependency conflicts between projects.
  • Containerization (Docker): packages code + dependencies + lightweight OS settings into one container → identical results on a laptop, a server, or the cloud.

Phase 3: Data Wrangling & Preprocessing (The 80% Rule)

تنضيف وتجهيز الداتا وقاعدة الـ 80%

The 80% Rule: analysts spend 80% of project time cleaning and preparing data and only 20% on modeling and evaluation. Raw data is like freshly harvested produce: dirty and needing washing before cooking. Goal: transform messy, inconsistent raw data into a clean, standardized, structured format. Model quality depends on input quality: "Garbage In, Garbage Out."

Data Cleaning→Data Transformation→Feature Engineering
Cleaning taskMethods
Missing values (Null / NaN)Deletion (if the missing proportion is negligible) · Imputation (mean, median, mode) · Flagging (an indicator variable telling the model the value was absent)

\[x' = \frac{x - x_{\min}}{x_{\max} - x_{\min}} \qquad\qquad z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}\]
\[\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1\]
\[\text{outlier if}\;\; x < Q_1 - 1.5\,\text{IQR} \;\;\text{or}\;\; x > Q_3 + 1.5\,\text{IQR}\]

The lecture names the methods (Min-Max, Z-score, IQR); the 1.5 × IQR fence and the formulas above are the standard textbook definitions. The book's checklist states Min-Max gives the range 0 to 1 and Z-score gives mean = 0, std = 1.

نرتّب القيم المعروفة (7 قيم):

\[\frac{165 + 200}{7} = \frac{365}{7} \approx 52.14\]

وده رقم مالوش علاقة بأعمار الناس دي خالص 😅

وأصغر قيمة دايمًا تبقى 0 وأكبر قيمة تبقى 1.

5.2

Parameters vs Hyperparameters & Tuning

الفرق بين parameters و hyperparameters وضبطهم

ParametersHyperparameters
Who sets them?the model learns them automatically from data during trainingyou (the data scientist) set them before training
Examplesweights in a neural network; slope and intercept in linear regressionRandom Forest: number of trees, max depth · K-Means: number of clusters K · NN: learning rate, hidden layers, batch size
Rolethe result of learningcontrol how the learning happens

Cake analogy (book): the algorithm is the recipe, the data is the ingredients, the parameters are the batter, and the hyperparameters are the oven settings (temperature, time, rack position) you must set before baking.

MethodHow it works
Grid Searchtests every combination in a predefined grid; exhaustive within the grid
Random Searchsamples random combinations; cheaper for large spaces
Cross-Validationcombined with either search to evaluate each setting reliably
\[\text{Grid models} = \prod (\text{values per hyperparameter})\]
\[3 \times 3 = 9 \;\;\text{models (book example)}\]

Wrong hyperparameters cause overfitting (memorizing the data) or underfitting (failing to learn). A learning rate that is too high may fail to learn; too low may take forever. Tuning finds the "sweet spot".

عالشرح بالعربيإعدادات الفرن
  • Parameters = اللي الموديل بيتعلمه لوحده (الأوزان، الـ slope).
  • Hyperparameters = اللي إنت بتختاره قبل التدريب (عدد الأشجار، K، الـ learning rate).
  • زي الكيكة: الوصفة = الخوارزمية، المكونات = الداتا، الخليط = parameters، وإعدادات الفرن = hyperparameters.
  • Grid Search بيجرّب كل التوافيق. لو عندك learning rate بـ 3 قيم وعدد أشجار بـ 3 قيم يبقى 3 × 3 = 9 موديلات.
  • Random Search بياخد عينة عشوائية من التوافيق.

فخ: «Number of clusters K is learned from data» ← غلط، K hyperparameter إنت بتحدده.

5.3

Model Evaluation Metrics & the Iterative Loop

مقاييس تقييم الموديل والرجوع للمراحل اللي قبل

ProblemMetricWhat it tells you
ClassificationAccuracyoverall % of correct predictions; misleading on imbalanced data
ClassificationPrecisionof all predicted "Fraud", how many were really fraud? (avoid false alarms)
ClassificationRecallof all actual frauds, how many did we catch? (avoid missed cases)
ClassificationF1-Scorebalance of precision and recall; high only if both are good
ClassificationAUC-ROC0.0 to 1.0 separation ability; 0.5 = random guessing, near 1.0 = excellent
RegressionMAEaverage absolute error in real units; treats all errors equally
RegressionRMSEsquares errors before averaging → punishes large errors heavily
RegressionR²share of variance explained; 1.0 perfect, 0.0 = no better than predicting the average

Imbalance example: if 99% of transactions are legitimate and 1% fraudulent, a "dumb" model that always says Legitimate is 99% accurate but catches zero fraud. That's why F1 and AUC-ROC matter.

\[\text{Accuracy} = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}\]
\[\text{Precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP} \qquad \text{Recall} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}\]
\[F_1 = \frac{2 \cdot \text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}\]
\[\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{n}\sum |y_i - \hat y_i| \qquad \text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum (y_i - \hat y_i)^2}\]
\[R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum (y_i - \hat y_i)^2}{\sum (y_i - \bar y)^2}\]

The lecture and book define each metric in words; the formulas above are the standard definitions that match those words.

The iterative loop
  • Poor evaluation metrics → return to Phase 3 to engineer stronger features.
  • Unexpected model errors → return to Phase 4 to re-examine distributions and anomalies.
🧮يلا نعرف نحل: مسائل التقييمConfusion matrix + MAE/RMSE
1
Classification: موديل fraud على 1000 عملية

الموديل قال fraud على 50 عملية، منهم 40 فعلًا fraud. وإجمالي الـ fraud الحقيقي 60. يبقى:

\[TP = 40,\qquad FP = 50 - 40 = 10\]
\[FN = 60 - 40 = 20,\qquad TN = 1000 - 40 - 10 - 20 = 930\]
\[\text{Accuracy} = \frac{40 + 930}{1000} = 0.97\]
\[\text{Precision} = \frac{40}{50} = 0.80 \qquad \text{Recall} = \frac{40}{60} \approx 0.667\]
\[F_1 = \frac{2(0.8)(0.667)}{0.8 + 0.667} = \frac{8}{11} \approx 0.727\]
الـ Accuracy عالية (97%) بس الموديل فوّت ثلث حالات الـ fraud (Recall 66.7%). ده بالظبط سبب إن الـ Accuracy مضللة في الداتا غير المتوازنة.
2
الموديل «الغبي» اللي دايمًا بيقول Legitimate

نفس الـ 1000 عملية (60 fraud):

\[TP = 0,\quad TN = 940 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{Accuracy} = \frac{940}{1000} = 0.94,\qquad \text{Recall} = \frac{0}{60} = 0\]
3
Regression: MAE و RMSE

الأسعار الحقيقية 100, 150, 200, 250 والمتوقعة 110, 140, 200, 300. الأخطاء:

\[e = (-10,\; 10,\; 0,\; -50)\]
\[\text{MAE} = \frac{10 + 10 + 0 + 50}{4} = 17.5\]
\[\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{100 + 100 + 0 + 2500}{4}} = \sqrt{675} \approx 25.98\]

الـ RMSE أكبر من الـ MAE بوضوح لأن فيه غلطة كبيرة واحدة (50)، والـ RMSE بيعاقب الأخطاء الكبيرة.
